**AI consulting in Stockholm** has a reputation problem. Too many engagements end with a heatmap of opportunities and a recommendation to “upskill the organisation.” The company already knew it was behind. It needed a first system.

Good consulting is the work that happens before and around a build: which use case, which data, which vendors, which risks, and who will run the thing on a Tuesday.

## What we will not sell as consulting

- A six-month transformation office with no software
- Training days that do not change a process
- Vendor bake-offs that ignore your actual tools
- Governance frameworks copied from a bank you are not

Those products exist because they are easy to staff. They are rarely what a 20-200 person company needs.

## A consulting sequence that earns the next invoice

**Week 1, inventory.** Interviews with the people who do the work. List of systems. List of constraints (legal, union, customer contracts).

**Week 2, one bet.** We pick a single use case with a measurable outcome. Everything else goes on a parking lot.

**Week 3, design.** Architecture, human review, and a build estimate a technical person can attack.

**Week 4, decide.** Build with us, build internally, or stop. Stopping is a successful consulting outcome.

After that, the engagement should become [implementation](/services#ai-agent-development) or it should end.

## Readiness is not a vibe

We look at four facts:

- Is there a process owner who can change the workflow?
- Is the data already digital, even if it is ugly?
- Can the company accept an 80% solution with a human on the last 20%?
- Is there budget for software, not only workshops?

If three of four are missing, the honest advice is to wait or to fix operations first.

## Stockholm-specific questions

Swedish companies often ask about the EU AI Act, GDPR, and whether they must host in Sweden. The useful answer is: classify the use case, list the personal data, list the subprocessors, and write the residual risk in language a CEO can repeat. Do not hide behind “we are compliant” as a slogan.

Public-sector and regulated buyers will also care about documentation. Consulting should produce artefacts they can reuse in procurement, not a private Notion that expires with the contractor.
