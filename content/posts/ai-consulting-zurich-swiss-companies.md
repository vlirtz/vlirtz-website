Zurich has no shortage of firms offering **AI consulting**. It has a much shorter list of vendors who can show a Swiss client a system in production, not a slide about one.

If you are evaluating an **AI software agency in Zurich**, or a remote European partner serving Zurich, the questions below separate the two.

## Start with data residency, not the model

Swiss financial services, insurance, and pharma companies usually ask about data location before they ask about the model. That order is correct. Before any pilot, a serious vendor should be able to answer:

- Where does customer data sit while the agent or workflow runs?
- Is the vendor scoping this against the Swiss FADP, GDPR, or both?
- Who has access to production data during development, and for how long?

If a vendor treats this as a legal afterthought rather than part of the architecture, that is a signal, not a technicality.

## A local office is not the point

Some of the best AI partners for Zurich companies are not based in Zurich. What matters more:

1. **Same time zone.** A partner in Stockholm, Munich, or Amsterdam shares Zurich's working hours. A partner eight time zones away does not, whatever their pitch deck says about "global delivery."
2. **Willingness to be on-site for what matters.** Kickoffs and key reviews benefit from a room. Weekly syncs do not need one.
3. **A named person who will build, not just sell.** Ask who writes the code and prompts, not just who presents.

## What a first engagement should look like

For Swiss companies, we recommend the same structure we use everywhere: a two-to-four week build or a four-week consulting sequence, scoped to one workflow with a measurable outcome, not a roadmap. See [how VLIRTZ works with Zurich](/locations/zurich) for the specifics of that engagement, including our default data-residency position for Swiss and EU clients.

## Questions worth asking any AI consulting firm in Zurich

- Can you show one Swiss or EU client reference we can actually contact?
- What happens to our data if we do not renew after the pilot?
- Who owns the prompts, policies, and credentials when the contract ends?
- How do you handle FADP and GDPR together, not as two separate checklists?

If the answers are vague, that is useful information too.
