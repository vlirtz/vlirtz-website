Amsterdam and Copenhagen founders tend to ask the same first question of any **AI software agency**: can you show me something that works, not a deck that explains why it will. This is the checklist we would want if we were hiring, written for both markets because the answer is nearly identical in each.

## The one-page scope, before any sales call

Write this before you talk to an agency, not after:

- The job a person does today that is slow, expensive, or inconsistent.
- The systems that job already touches (CRM, ERP, spreadsheets, inboxes).
- The date you need a first usable version.
- What you will explicitly not build in this phase.

Send that page to every **AI consulting** or **AI software agency** you shortlist in Amsterdam, Copenhagen, or anywhere else. Agencies that rewrite it into a 30-slide proposal are selling process. Agencies that hand back a smaller, sharper scope have usually shipped before.

## What Amsterdam scale-ups tend to prioritize

Dutch scale-ups we talk to want a working prototype before a strategy conversation. The order that works:

1. A thin, scripted version of the workflow, no model involved yet.
2. An agent layered on top for the decisions that actually need judgement.
3. A roadmap, once something real exists to extend.

## What Copenhagen and Nordic buyers tend to prioritize

Danish and wider Nordic companies we work with share a pattern with Swedish ones: skepticism about AI vendors, and a strong preference for a small, reversible pilot over a large commitment. A serious agency should offer a two-to-four week first engagement with a defined stop point, not a retainer you have to actively cancel.

## Questions worth asking either way

- Who will actually build this, and can we meet them before signing?
- What do we own (prompts, code, credentials) if we do not continue past the pilot?
- How do you handle GDPR for this specific workflow, not just as a policy statement?
- What does "done" look like for the first milestone, in writing?
