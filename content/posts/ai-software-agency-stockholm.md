Stockholm has no shortage of people who will talk about AI. It has a much shorter list of teams who will sit in your stack, pick one workflow, and leave you with something your staff still uses ninety days later.

If you are searching for an **AI software agency in Stockholm**, you are usually trying to answer three questions at once: what should we build, who should build it, and how do we avoid a pilot that dies in a slide deck.

## Start with a job, not a model

The useful first conversation is not “GPT versus Claude.” It is “which weekly task is slow, expensive, or inconsistent?” Good candidates are work that already has a paper trail: inbound leads, support tickets, invoice exceptions, research that a person copies between tools, or a process that only one employee knows how to run.

An agency that begins with a model name, a transformation programme, or a 40-page opportunity map is selling you time. Ask them to name one workflow, the systems it touches, and what “done” looks like in a month.

## What a Stockholm AI software agency should actually deliver

Look for a partner who can do all three of these, not just one:

1. **Advise.** Decide what is worth automating under Swedish and EU constraints (data, vendors, the EU AI Act where it applies).
2. **Build.** Ship an agent or application that calls your tools, not a chat window on an island.
3. **Operate.** Leave logs, a human review step, and a person on your side who knows how to change a prompt or a rule.

## Questions that separate a builder from a brochure

Ask any agency you shortlist:

- Which of your last three projects is still in production?
- Who owns the prompts, evaluations, and vendor accounts when the contract ends?
- How do you keep a human in the loop for actions that spend money or write to a customer?
- What data leaves Sweden or the EU, and why?
- Can you show the first milestone in writing, with a date and an owner?

If the answers are vague, keep looking. Stockholm’s market is small enough that you can ask for a named reference or a recorded walkthrough of a live system.

## Local matters more than people admit

An AI project dies in the details: Fortnox or Visma, BankID, Swedish-language support, public procurement habits, and the fact that many mid-market companies will not put customer data in a US-only tool without a story they can tell their board. A Stockholm-based team should already know those constraints.

That does not mean the work has to stay in one city. It means the first workshop should not start with “we will figure out GDPR later.”

## A sensible first engagement

The first paid piece of work should be narrow:

- one process
- one success metric (hours saved, reply time, qualified meetings)
- access to the real tools, not a demo tenant
- a two-to-four week build, then a decision to extend or stop
